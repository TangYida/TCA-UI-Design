Physical AI: Why China Believes AI Needs a Body

Today’s AI can compose poems, generate code, and design slides with ease—yet it still cannot turn an elderly person over in bed.

By The China Academy Editorial Desk

Artificial intelligence is leaving the screen. In laboratories, factories and hospitals, researchers are asking what changes when a model must perceive weight, resistance, uncertainty and human vulnerability in the physical world.

China’s wager on embodied intelligence joins computation to manufacturing capacity. The difficult questions are therefore social as well as technical: who owns the machines, who maintains them, and whether automation reduces difficult work or merely redistributes its burdens.
